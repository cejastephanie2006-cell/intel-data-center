    My notes

    What the data told me, one question at a time

    1

    Who makes more than they use?

    Five regions are net producers. Mid-Atlantic leads at about 31M MWh and the Northwest is second at about 22M.

    2

    When is Mid-Atlantic's surplus biggest?

    July 2022, when generation pulls furthest above demand.

    3

    Who runs greenest?

    Northwest, Central and California. Big river systems, a wind corridor and year-round sunshine give them a head start.

    4

    Who does both?

    Only the Northwest and Central are net producers that also rank top 5 for renewables.

    5

    When does California's wind pick up?

    Hours 10 to 22, peaking near 3 PM at about +9.5% per hour. Early mornings are when it slows down.

    6

    NW vs CAL, side by side

    California's renewables spike at midday and drop off at night. The Northwest's line stays almost flat.

    MY RECOMMENDATIONBuild in the Northwest.

    A big energy surplus, the highest renewable share in the country, and hydropower that keeps running after the sun goes down.

    Data & method

    What's behind every number

    Use my real data

    Drop energy_dataset.csv here or choose it. It stays in your browser.

    Demo mode. Net production and renewable % for all 13 regions were read off my Tableau charts, so they're close but rounded. The power mix, monthly trends and hourly curves are sample values shaped to look realistic. Load the CSV and every chart switches to the real numbers.

    Calculations. Net production = SUM(Net Generation) minus SUM(Demand). Renewable % = (Wind + Solar + Hydropower and Pumped Storage) / Net Generation. Steady ratio = a region's lowest hour of renewable output divided by its highest. Site fit = net producer + top 5 renewable + steady ratio of 0.75 or more.
